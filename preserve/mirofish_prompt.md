# MiroFish prompt (paste with preserve_seed.md)

Simulate how Indian buyers react to Preserve Archive 001, a numbered 001-100 limited release of a hand-painted tin can with a T-shirt and a marigold seed, priced at Rs 1,099, sold as a 100% preorder through Instagram.

Create two groups of simulated people:
- Group A, about 60%: Indian streetwear and sneaker collectors aged 19-28 who follow labels like Gully Labs and Comet and are used to scarce, numbered drops.
- Group B, about 40%: people who would buy it as a gift for a partner or friend, mainly because they like the idea of growing a plant.

Run the simulation in three phases:
1. Discovery: people see a faceless Instagram reel and the bio link. Show which kinds of people stop, which scroll past, and why.
2. Purchase: who preorders at Rs 1,099, who hesitates, and what blocks them. Pay particular attention to paying before the product exists, trust in a brand with no history, and a 3-4 week wait.
3. Archive 002 announcement, about 8 weeks after Archive 001 ships: who buys again, who does not, and why. Separate Group A from Group B. Include buyers whose marigold died and buyers whose marigold bloomed.

Report:
- Preorder rate and repeat rate for each group, as ranges and not single numbers
- The 5 most likely reasons a buyer does NOT come back for Archive 002
- The single change that would most raise Archive 002 repeat purchase
- Where the simulated people disagree most with the assumptions in the seed document
- What Preserve cannot know from this simulation and must test with real people

Use at most 40 simulation rounds for this run.
