# Narrative Report: Product team workshop on Preserve pain points and improvements

> **Read this first.** Everything below is an **LLM simulation of a team discussion**. Twelve AI agents played the roles of a product team. Nothing in this report is customer evidence, market data or legal advice. The fixes, rankings, costs and plans are **hypotheses and suggestions** that still need testing with real people. Anything marked UNKNOWN in the source material is still unknown. Every legal point needs checking by a qualified professional.

## What Preserve Is and What This Workshop Was

**Preserve** is a small, early, founder-led brand that sells on Instagram (a reel plus a bio link). Its first release, **Archive 001**, is 100 numbered units (001 to 100). Each unit has a hand-painted tin can, a T-shirt and a marigold seed, priced at Rs 1,099 (about Rs 1,09,900 gross if the full run sells). It is sold only as a preorder, and buyers wait 3-4 weeks after paying. The brand has no history and the founder does not show their face. The next release, **Archive 002**, is planned for about 8 weeks after Archive 001 ships. Its product and price are UNKNOWN.

A first simulation (sim_6ae0fd697403) played out how an imagined audience might react to Archive 001 and found **9 pain points**. This second simulation is an **internal workshop** in which 12 team roles go through those 9 pain points one by one. For each one they argue, propose a fix with an owner, a rough cost and a real-world test, and at the end they produce a ranked priority list, a 30-day plan and a log of what they still disagree about. The Head of Product chaired. The ground rules (decision D0, round 1) were: UNKNOWNs stay unknown, proposals are labelled as proposals, every fix needs an owner, a rough cost and a real-world test, and anything expensive gets flagged.

### The 12 roles

## Synopsis

The workshop opened with the Founder turning a painted tin over in their hands: "I still believe in two things: the number on the tin and the marigold inside it. Everything else is on the table. Just don't bring me invented numbers." Within minutes Research had set the tone for the week. The figures from the first simulation ("5 of 6", "0 of 5") came from "36 LLM personas and scripted marigold outcomes", so they showed a direction worth testing and nothing more. The Designer opened an empty Archive 001 tin, with no card and no instructions, and left it on the table. The Plant Specialist put a seed packet next to it: "A seed, and nothing telling anyone what to do with it."

The Founder wanted Archive 002 first, arguing that naming it would quiet everything else. The Head of Product refused and put it last, saying an announcement on top of a dead seed with no remedy is "the same blind prepay with a nicer first chapter". From there the team went through the pain points in order. The dead-seed problem got a combined bundle: lid card, seed storage, germination test, bloom-or-not follow-up and replacement flow. The refund argument (Founder: deal with it in DMs; CX: public written promise; Finance: cap the exposure) ended with the Founder making a bet: the remedy line goes public unless a waitlist A/B test shows it lowers signups. Ops won the delivery-date argument by refusing Growth's "ships in 21 days" until painting had actually been timed.

The emotional peak of the week came in round 9. Community stood next to the board and told the Founder that someone had "paid Rs 1,099 to a page with no face, no history and no product". The Founder pushed back: "The day it's my face, it's a personal brand selling tins." Brand found the way through ("faceless and anonymous aren't the same thing"), and in round 10 the Founder agreed to show their hands, put a business name on the terms and have support replies signed by a named person, but kept their face off for Archive 002. Community logged the face question as "open, not lost". Over the next rounds the team settled "never remade, 100 means 100", a working identity line ("A numbered object that grows"), a spec card and a plan to collect real evidence on collectors versus gift buyers.

The second confrontation came in round 19. The Founder spilled coffee on the scoring grid while defending the 8-week date: "Faceless and late is exactly the story the skeptics told." Ops did not name a number but pointed to the Founder's own stopwatch run: "you know it's not the number you had in your head." Growth, usually the one pushing for speed, offered the compromise: open a waitlist now, but open the preorder link only when every launch gate is green. In round 20 the Founder conceded: "It took me longer than I told myself. Ops is right." The 8-week target became internal only, and the public date became an output of a five-item launch gate. The Founder also committed to announcing any slip in their own words.

The workshop closed with a ranked list initialled by the Founder, a week-by-week 30-day plan with every role's commitments, and an "OPEN - NOT RESOLVED" box holding 8 disagreements, each tied to the test that will settle it. CX deleted the "message me" saved reply. Ops restarted the stopwatch on the remaining pilot tins. Research had the last word on what the week was worth: "Nothing decided this week is evidence yet. It's 24 well-argued hypotheses with tests attached."

## The 9 Pain Points: What Each Role Said, Where They Disagreed, and the Proposed Fix

The pain points were discussed in this order (D1): PP1, PP2, PP9, PP4, PP6, PP7, PP5, PP8, PP3. Each pain point was later grouped into a fix bundle F1-F9 (D12, round 18). Simulated figures from the first report are hypotheses, not data.

### PP1: Dead marigold with no remedy or growing guide (rounds 3-4, became F1)
*First-simulation signal (simulated): buyers whose marigold bloomed mostly said they would buy again; those whose seed died did not.*

- **Head of Product** split the problem into PREVENT, REMEDY and KNOW.
- **Plant Specialist:** proposed a real germination test on the actual seed lot, publishing a figure only once it has been measured and labelled as Preserve's own test. Also a side test of sowing in the tin versus a pot, because the grow method is UNKNOWN.
- **Designer:** proposed a printed card inside the tin lid, with the number and story on the front and growing steps on the back. It adds no painting time.
- **CX:** proposed a dead-seed flow: the buyer sends a photo and gets a replacement within a set time, offered retroactively to Archive 001 buyers.
- **Research:** proposed a bloom-or-not follow-up message to every real Archive 001 buyer.
- **Ops:** asked where the seeds were stored ("Heat, damp, how long in transit?") and added a storage log, with stored and fresh seed compared in the test.
- **Growth:** wanted to film the germination test as a time-lapse.
- **Finance:** "I just don't sign blank cheques." Will model replacement exposure as claims × (seed + packing + postage) once Ops has quotes.
- **Community:** the follow-up should come from a person, "not a survey".
- **Legal:** if a test result is published, say how it was done and do not present it as a guarantee (verify the wording with a professional).
- **Disagreements:** Research against Growth: publish the time-lapse whatever the result, or don't film it (Research's rule was adopted). Brand (voice and look of the card) against Designer (function first). Plant against Finance on spare seeds, which were parked until test and cost data exist.
- **Fix (D2):** the PP1 bundle of lid card, storage fix, germination test, bloom-or-not follow-up and dead-seed replacement flow, with spare seeds parked.

### PP2: No refund or replacement policy (rounds 5-6, became F2)
*Signal: "message me" did not count as a policy for anyone in the first simulation.*

- **Founder:** "I'd rather handle it person to person. In DMs I can actually be generous."
- **CX:** proposed a three-line public promise: replacement seed if no sprout (with a photo), a refund option if the order isn't dispatched by the stated date, and replace or refund for damage.
- **Finance:** line one is fine. Line two is the problem, because the worst case is the whole Rs 1,09,900. Wants replacement-first and a cap.
- **Ops:** "you've written a refund cheque against my guess" until painting is timed.
- **Growth:** sided with CX, because a remedy line is a conversion asset.
- **Community:** "Being generous in DMs only helps the people brave enough to DM a faceless account."
- **Brand:** if it must exist, one short paragraph in the brand's voice, "not an airline".
- **Plant:** "Every seed the card and the storage fix save is a claim you never pay for."
- **Legal:** buyers may already have consumer-protection rights that wording cannot remove, and a refund tied to a date only works if the date is real. Verify with a professional.
- **Research:** designed the test, a waitlist A/B with and without the remedy line.
- **Disagreements:** Founder (DMs) against CX (public), and Finance (cap, replacement-first) against CX (refund for non-dispatch). Finance's sign-off on the damage refund stayed open until round 21.
- **Fix (D3 + D4):** the Founder agreed the line goes public unless the A/B shows it lowers signups. Merged promise: seed failure means a replacement with a photo; damage means replace or refund (buyer's choice); non-dispatch means a refund option against an Ops-approved date; preorders are capped to what that date can cover; wording goes through Legal and a professional. In round 21 (D15) Finance signed the damage line, with a review after Archive 002 and on condition that Ops logs every damaged unit.

### PP9: 3-4 week wait with no delivery date (rounds 7-8, became F3)
*Signal: a few simulated buyers raised it, and gift occasions were at risk.*

- **Growth:** wanted "ships in 21 days" in the reel hook.
- **Ops:** refused. Painting time had never been measured. Proposed a timed 10-tin painting and packing pilot, then a dispatch date with a buffer.
- **CX:** proposed weekly order updates through the wait (painting started, half done, packed, shipped) with a bench photo.
- **Research:** proposed an optional "Is this a gift? Is there a date it must arrive by?" question.
- **Plant:** flagged seasonality, since the sowing window could affect the Archive 002 timeline.
- **Finance:** backed Ops' buffered date as insurance against refunds.
- **Disagreement:** Growth (date in the hook now) against Ops (no date until timed). Ops won.
- **Fix (D5):** Ops runs the pilot and signs a buffered date, which goes on the waitlist page, the bio link and the remedy promise. CX sends weekly updates. Growth may use the date only after Ops signs it.

### PP4: Prepay trust gap: faceless founder, no history, paying before the product exists (rounds 9-10, became F6)

- **Founder:** "No face. That was never an accident... The number on the tin is the identity."
- **Community:** a visible human (the face, or at least a named person) closes the gap. "Your words were the proof. Imagine what your hands could do."
- **Brand:** "faceless and anonymous aren't the same thing". Proposed hands-not-face content from the painting bench.
- **CX:** "Even if you stay faceless, support can't." Signs replies with their own name.
- **Legal:** proposed a business name and contact route on the preorder terms, plus a consent rule for reposting buyer content (verify with a professional).
- **Growth:** real buyers, blurred receipts and unboxings as free funnel content.
- **Research:** only 6 of 28 simulated non-buyers cited trust, so whether a face would move real buyers is unknown. Test hands against mystique first.
- **Plant and Designer:** a seed tray "is proof that doesn't need a face". Shoot the content during the Ops pilot so it costs nothing extra.
- **Ops:** "Film around the clock, not instead of it."
- **Disagreement:** Founder's face against Community. This stayed open.
- **Fix (D6 + D7):** a trust stack: hands-at-the-bench content (Brand + Community), business name and contact on the terms (Legal, verify with a professional), named support (CX) and consented real-buyer proof (Community). Tested with a hands reel against a mystique-only reel, measured on waitlist signups. The face question is logged as open.

### PP6: Scarcity alone does not win collectors (rounds 11-13, became F7)
*Signal: the simulated collector lead bought only after a reseller rumour was answered.*

- **Community:** an opt-in public number registry (number, handle, optional unboxing or bloom photo) and a pinned "rumours, answered" post in the Founder's own words.
- **Brand:** a public "never remade, 100 means 100" statement.
- **Designer:** the same number on the tin and on the lid card.
- **Ops:** damage replacements get the same number repainted, never a 101st unit.
- **Growth:** a live, true "X of 100 claimed" hook, and earlier pushed countdown and "LAST CHANCE" hooks.
- **Brand against Growth:** no shouted urgency hooks. This became an open disagreement, though the two made up later.
- **Legal:** urgency and scarcity claims must be accurate when seen, with no static counts in evergreen reels, and the count must match the preorder cap (verify with a professional).
- **Research:** a 3-variant hook test (live count / registry proof / bench process) measured on signups, not views.
- **CX:** the registry should show dead seeds and remedies too, not only blooms.
- **Fix (D8 + D9):** the Founder decided "never remade, 100 means 100" (wording checked by Legal). A live true count is allowed and tested. "Last chance" is only allowed if literally true. Shouted urgency was later cut for this cycle (D14).

### PP7: Tin vs plant identity split (rounds 13-14, became F8)
*Signal: one simulated buyer called it "a candle shop, not a drop", and some valued the tin over the plant.*

- **Founder:** both, as one object.
- **Brand:** one must lead. Proposed that the numbered drop leads and the plant is "the reveal inside".
- **Designer:** the unboxing order tells the story: number card (number up), flip for grow steps and remedy, then the seed, then the tee.
- **Plant:** sowing in the tin is untested, so the guide should say to sow in a pot and keep the tin. The Founder accepted this.
- **Research:** a framing test with Cut A (number first) against Cut B (bloom first), plus a "for you or for someone else?" question.
- **Disagreement:** Founder (one object) against Brand (drop leads). This stayed open.
- **Fix (D10):** draft identity line "A numbered object that grows". Drop leads, plant is the reveal, sow in a pot and keep the tin, layered unboxing. Pending the framing test.

### PP5: Tee fabric, sizes, payment method and tin size unanswered (round 15, became F4)

- **Founder:** "I deferred these in the simulation... That was a mistake... But I'm not making up a fabric to fill a box."
- **Head of Product:** "This item isn't about invention. It's about deciding."
- **Designer:** a spec card published before the Archive 002 link opens (fabric, size chart in cm, fit, colours, tin dimensions, grow method, payment method), with every line measured or confirmed.
- **Ops:** order tees to the sizes buyers pick at checkout once the preorder closes, with exchanges only if stock exists.
- **CX:** "Sizes means exchanges." Wants a clear rule in the remedy promise.
- **Finance:** pick a payment method that gives buyers receipts and allows clean refunds. Finance will compare options and fees.
- **Legal:** descriptions must match what ships, and clothing and seeds may have labelling requirements (ask a professional).
- **Brand:** same typography as the lid card, "no tables that look like a marketplace listing".
- **Research:** count spec questions in DMs and comments, Archive 002 against Archive 001.
- **Disagreement:** CX (clear exchange promise, spare tees) against Ops (exchange only if in stock). This stayed open.

### PP8: Gift buyers rarely repeat; collector vs gift split untested (rounds 16-17, became F9)
*Signal: the first simulation assumed roughly 60% collectors and 40% gift buyers (unproven). The Founder's survey says gift buyers are the main group, but its details are UNKNOWN.*

- **Founder:** the survey shows gift buyers lead. The Founder felt their "real survey was being interrogated while the simulation got a report".
- **Research:** the survey can't be verified until they see who was asked and how. Research called both sources unverified.
- **Community:** reframed repeat business: the gift recipient can become the next buyer, through a recipient follow-up or registry invite.
- **Plant:** if gift buyers lead, the plant fix matters even more.
- **Fix (D11, P8a, P8b):** the Founder shares the survey questions and method privately with Research, and the results stay with the Founder until Research reports what they can prove. Research adds a buyer-type question, 10 short interviews with real Archive 001 buyers, and bloom-or-not results split by group. The recipient follow-up was parked until consent wording is reviewed (D14).

### PP3: Archive 002 product and price unnamed (rounds 17-20, became F5)
*Signal: tied for the most-cited blocker in the first simulation.*

- **Founder:** proposed keeping the three-part format (object, tee, living thing), with product and price decided within 3 weeks. Later added a proposal to pick something that reuses existing suppliers and the painting method.
- **Head of Product:** a five-item launch gate. The preorder link opens only when product, price, spec card, remedy promise and an Ops-approved dispatch date are all public.
- **Finance:** a price guardrail method (unit cost + remedy reserve + payment fees, with margin, from real quotes) and a price test, with no number given.
- **Ops:** 8 weeks only holds if Archive 002 reuses Archive 001's suppliers and method. F5 is "the only row that could break the timeline".
- **Plant:** "The plant doesn't care about promises." A new living thing chosen in week 3 leaves no time for a test cycle.
- **Growth:** announce sooner, then proposed splitting "announce" from "open".
- **Research:** the tests need about 2-3 weeks of real traffic.
- **Disagreements:** Founder (hold 8 weeks) against Ops, Plant and Head of Product (the date follows the gates), and Growth (rank F5 higher) against Plant and CX (F1/F2 first).
- **Fix (D13):** 8 weeks stays an internal target. The waitlist goes live now. The preorder link opens only when all five gates are green. If the week-4 gate review fails, the slip is announced publicly with a new date signed by Ops.

## What Stays UNKNOWN, What Is Only a Proposal, and What Needs a Professional

**Still UNKNOWN (nothing in the workshop changed this):**

- Tee fabric, sizes, fit and colours; tin size; payment method.
- Archive 002 product and price.
- Hand-painting time per tin and therefore any real dispatch date.

- The real collector vs gift-buyer split, and the details of the Founder's survey.

## Character Arcs

### Founder / CEO

## Pivotal Scenes

**Round 5-6: the remedy standoff.** The Founder said a public refund line would invite "How do I get my money back" as the first comment. CX read the three promises aloud. Finance put the calculator down hard: "The worst case is the whole Rs 1,09,900." Growth unexpectedly sided with CX. It ended with the Founder betting on a test rather than instinct (D3).

**Round 9-10: the face.** Community stood at the board: "someone paid Rs 1,099 to a page with no face, no history and no product." The Founder answered: "You're asking me to swap the thing that makes it collectible for the thing that makes it ordinary." Brand split the difference ("Hands, the bench, the brush, the paint under someone's nails"), and the Founder, turning the brush over, agreed to everything except showing their face. CX changed their reply signature from "Team Preserve" to their own name in front of the room.

**Round 19-20: the date.** Coffee hit the grid. Ops: "you know it's not the number you had in your head when you said eight weeks." Growth proposed the waitlist-now, link-later split. A round later the Founder spoke to the tins, "Ops is right", and handed the date to the gates. Finance rewrote "refund risk from missed date - HIGH" as "LOW if gate holds".

**Round 22: the list.** The Founder initialled the ranking: "I own number 4 and I'll be measured on it like everyone else." Growth: "Record my dissent... But I'll ship the waitlist and the tests either way."

**Round 24: the close.** Brand handed tin 001 back: "Still numbered. Still yours. Just a bit easier to believe now." CX deleted the "message me" reply. Ops started the stopwatch.

## Themes

- **Proposals, not proof.** The workshop kept separating what is known from what is guessed. Every fix carries a test, and the ranking itself has a footnote to re-rank on real data.
- **Accountability without a face.** The trust problem was split into parts and solved mostly without the Founder's face: hands, a business name, named support and written promises.
- **Prevention before apology.** The top fix reduces dead seeds rather than just compensating for them. "Every seed the card and the storage fix save is a claim you never pay for."
- **The date as an output, not an input.** Ops' stopwatch and Plant's seasons beat the 8-week promise, and the gate structure turned a fight into a rule.
- **Mystique versus visible trust.** Brand and the Founder found that some mystique survives honesty, such as a numbered card on top or hands rather than a face.
- **Disagree and commit.** Eight disagreements stayed open, each tied to the evidence that will settle it, rather than being settled by rank or volume.

## Divergences from Source

The source material gave pain points and UNKNOWNs but no answers. Everything the team produced is new: the PREVENT/REMEDY/KNOW split, the lid card, the merged remedy promise with a preorder cap, the timed painting pilot, "hands, not face", "never remade, 100 means 100", the number registry, "A numbered object that grows", the five-item launch gate and the split between announcing and opening. In the simulation the Founder gave up the public 8-week date but kept their face off camera. The source treated gift buyers as fragile non-repeaters, and the team reframed the gift recipient as a possible next buyer. No source UNKNOWN was filled in with a fact. Where the team chose something (for example "sow in a pot"), it is labelled a proposal pending a test.

## Methodology Note

This narrative was generated by a multi-agent simulation where 12 AI characters interacted across 24 rounds. Each character was given a persona derived from the source material, including personality traits, motivations, relationships and initial stances, and made its own decisions about dialogue and actions. The resulting story is emergent, not pre-scripted.

**What this means for using it:** this is an **LLM simulation of a team discussion**, not a record of a real meeting, real customer evidence or legal advice. The simulated figures quoted from the first report (such as "6 of 28") come from AI personas, not real buyers. Cost tiers are rough, imagined estimates with no real quotes behind them. Treat the ranked list, plan and disagreements log as a **structured set of hypotheses and suggestions** to test with real people and, for legal points, to check with a qualified professional. Per-round tension values in report_data.json are estimates derived from round sentiment and conflict. This report draws on all 12 persona files, the final world state, the structured decisions and proposals recorded in all 24 rounds, and the full text of rounds 1, 2, 3, 5, 9, 10, 15, 18, 19, 20, 22, 23 and 24.
